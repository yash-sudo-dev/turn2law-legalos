Section 01

Executive summary

Turn2Law is a legal operating system for the two people the legal industry serves worst: the founder who cannot afford a lawyer on retainer, and the lawyer who cannot afford a bench of paralegals.

It is not a marketplace layered over the old system. It is one platform where human expertise and AI capability are parts of the same product, organised into three pillars. The discipline is simple: if a feature does not fit a pillar, it does not ship. A founder's compliance data and a lawyer's case data run on shared infrastructure, so every part of the platform makes the others stronger.

The three pillars

Pillar 01 Legal Services is human-delivered legal, compliance, and IP work, already anchored by a live client base. Pillar 02 DocEngine turns plain-language prompts into India-specific legal documents in minutes. Pillar 03 Introspector is an AI research and strategy bench for practising lawyers. Pillars 01 and 02 sell to founders. Pillar 03 sells to litigators, at a higher trust bar.

Where we are

MCA registered as Effivia Turn2Law Legal Pvt. Ltd., incubated at SRM DEI and the Bootstrappers Research Council, with an in-house team of lawyers, a CA, and a CS, and 5+ startups already registered and under active compliance: ICEBRKR, Sentinel Layer, Serene Neurotech, Motioncomm, Withera Labs.

What to build first

Phase 1 hardens Legal Services and DocEngine for a first cohort behind a secure, multi-tenant foundation and a public marketing site. Introspector enters a closed pilot in Phase 2. The three pillars unify on one account system in Phase 3.

Primary success questionIf this document went to a capable product and engineering team today, they should know exactly what Turn2Law needs to become, how it should look and behave, how it should be built and secured, and in what order to deliver it.

Section 04

Product architecture: the three pillars

Buyer disciplinePillars 01 and 02 sell to founders. Pillar 03 sells to litigators. Different buyer, different sales motion, and a higher trust bar for Pillar 03: a case-research error in a criminal matter is a far more serious failure mode than a flawed template. Keep this distinction deliberate in copy, pricing, and QA.

Pillar 01 · Legal Services: "Done right, not just fast."

Human-delivered legal, compliance, and IP work through in-house lawyers, a CA, and a CS. Catalogue: incorporation and registration (Pvt Ltd, OPC, LLP, Section 8, conversions). Name and brand protection (RUN and SPICe+ Part A, trademark search, filing, opposition, renewals, copyright, patent assistance). Regulatory and tax registrations (GST, MSME and Udyam, IEC, FSSAI, Shops and Establishment, Professional Tax). Ongoing statutory compliance (ROC filings, board resolutions, registers, DIN, auditor appointment, AGM, GST returns). Corporate and fundraising advisory (founders' agreement, ESOP, cap table, term sheet review, FEMA and RBI, FC-GPR). Employment and HR legal (contracts, POSH, PF and ESI, labour advisory). Disputes and recovery (legal notices, payment recovery, mediation referral). Retainer and closure (Virtual GC, strike-off, winding up).

Pillar 02 · DocEngine: "Prompt in. Drafted document out."

Plain-language input becomes a well-drafted, India-specific legal document in minutes, with the option to bring in a Turn2Law lawyer when stakes rise. Core engine: prompt-to-document, guided Q&A for founders unfamiliar with legal terms, an India-specific clause library updated as law changes. Template library (13+): NDA (mutual and one-way), MOU, founders' agreement, employment, consultancy, vendor and service agreements, privacy policy and terms, non-compete and non-solicit, IP assignment, shareholders' agreement, term sheet, lease, loan and promissory note. Intelligence: AI risk flagging on one-sided clauses, smart clause suggestions, a plain-language explainer beside every clause. Collaboration: multi-party review, e-signature, version history, PDF and DOCX export.

Pillar 03 · Introspector: "The lawyer's missing bench."

An AI research and strategy agent for practising lawyers. Case intake and organisation: digital case file, document repository, hearing and timeline tracker. Legal research: precedent search across judgments, statute and section recommendation across IPC and BNS, CrPC and BNSS, and the Evidence Act, a natural-language research assistant, a citation generator for filings. Case strategy: argument frameworks, counter-argument maps, cross-examination banks, deposition summarisation. Practice support: multi-user collaboration, a privileged document vault, outcome pattern analytics.

Responsible-use rule, mandatory on all Introspector surfacesThe research and analytics support a lawyer's judgment. They do not replace it. Section recommendations and outcome patterns must be reviewed by qualified counsel before use in any filing or court.

Legacy names

Earlier materials used LawGPT and ComplianceOS. ComplianceOS maps to the compliance calendar inside Pillar 01. LawGPT's founder Q&A is now DocEngine's guided Q&A layer. DocEngine keeps its name as Pillar 02.

Section 09

DocEngine (Pillar 02)

Document lifecycle: upload and ingestion, file-type validation, OCR for scans, text extraction, classification, metadata extraction, clause identification, summarisation, risk and inconsistency detection, contract comparison, version control, drafting, template and clause-library management, redlining, comments, approvals, e-signature, export to PDF and DOCX, retention and deletion, activity logs.

Behaviour

AI-generated edits are presented as suggestions a user reviews, accepts, rejects, and audits. They are never applied silently. Automated issue spotting is distinguished from conclusions that require a qualified professional. Supported formats, size limits, processing statuses, previews, and side-by-side comparison are defined per file type with explicit error handling.

SafetyProtect against prompt injection via uploaded documents, data leakage between customers, and hallucinated clauses. A model's confidence score is never treated as proof of legal accuracy.
Section 10

Introspector (Pillar 03)

The lawyer's bench. Design scenario: a lawyer takes a criminal case with the next hearing eight months away. Between now and then someone must research similar cases, map applicable sections, organise the file, and build the argument. Introspector is built to be that bench.

Findings model

Every finding carries severity, supporting evidence, source location, confidence limitations, reviewer status, and a remediation or next-action workflow. Findings are never presented as guaranteed legal conclusions.

Boundary with DocEngine

DocEngine drafts and reviews documents for founders. Introspector researches and organises cases for lawyers. Shared primitives, document storage and clause and section references, are one service. The two products do not duplicate drafting or research surfaces.

Responsible useResearch and analytics support counsel's judgment and never replace it. This line appears on every Introspector surface and in every exported report.

Section 19

Privacy and Indian regulatory

Turn2Law processes sensitive legal, financial, personal, and business documents, so privacy is an architectural principle, not a policy footnote.

Privacy by design

Data classification, minimisation, and purpose limitation. Consent and notices where required. Data ownership and access. Retention and deletion. User export and account deletion. Backup-deletion limits. Data residency in India. A named subprocessor list. Privacy impact assessments. Breach response. Periodic access reviews. A ban on using production data in development or testing.

AI data handling

Specify whether user data is sent to third-party models, provider retention and training policies, customer-controlled AI settings where feasible, redaction before model calls, and strict separation of customer data across retrieval indexes so one user's conversation can never retrieve another's.

Do not overclaimDo not claim zero-knowledge or end-to-end encryption, or that data is never used for model training, unless the architecture and provider contracts actually support it.

Regulatory obligations, validate with counsel

Likely applicable: the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and its rules and commencement provisions, the Information Technology Act and rules, CERT-In incident-reporting directions, consumer-protection and e-commerce requirements, payment obligations tied to the chosen model, copyright and database licensing, electronic-record and e-signature requirements, contractual confidentiality, and professional-conduct rules for lawyers, CAs, and CS professionals. The precise obligations depend on Turn2Law's business model, data flows, professional arrangements, and customer types, and must be confirmed by qualified Indian legal, privacy, and security professionals. This PRD is not a substitute for that advice.

Section 20

AI safety and evaluation

AI output is decision support, and the product says so plainly. Quality is measured, not assumed.

Evaluation covers legal accuracy, citation correctness, source freshness, retrieval relevance, hallucination rate, refusal quality, prompt-injection resistance, cross-tenant leakage, reproducibility and version tracking, human review, model-provider changes, and cost and latency. Each has a benchmark set, test cases, a threshold, a release gate, monitoring, and a rollback path.

Adversarial tests use malicious documents, conflicting legal sources, outdated laws, fabricated citations, ambiguous questions, and attempts to extract another customer's information. A finding that cannot be grounded in a verifiable source is refused or escalated, not answered with false confidence.
